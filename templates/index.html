    📑 Capstone Viva Defense: Small-Sample Regime Justification

    1. The Core Scientific Premise

    In controlled agricultural drought studies, procuring certified ground-truth imagery under measured volumetric soil moisture levels is highly labor-intensive, naturally leading to a small-sample regime ($N=55$). Deep Convolutional Neural Networks (e.g. ResNet, MobileNet) contain millions of unconstrained parameters, leading to catastrophic empirical overfitting when $N < 100$.

    2. Feature Engineering vs Black-Box Convolutions

    Rather than forcing a neural network to infer geometry from scratch, we explicitly inject agronomic domain knowledge through 15 biophysical descriptors:

    • Excess Green Index (ExG): $2G - R - B$ — isolates vegetative contrast from soil/shadows.
    • Green Leaf Index (GLI): $\frac{2G - R - B}{2G + R + B}$ — quantifies chlorophyll degradation during incipient drought.
    • Convex Hull Solidity: $\frac{\text{Area}}{\text{Convex Area}}$ — captures leaf margin involution and curling caused by turgor pressure drop.
    • Contour Compactness: $\frac{4\pi \times \text{Area}}{\text{Perimeter}^2}$ — measures cellular flaccidity and border irregularity.

    3. Why SVM (RBF) Outperformed Random Forest & Boosting

    In tabular settings where the number of features $p=15$ and samples $N=55$, Support Vector Machines with an RBF kernel project samples into an infinite-dimensional Hilbert space with margin maximization. The $L_2$ regularization parameter $C$ strictly bounds the VC-dimension, preventing the model from fitting high-frequency noise that tree ensembles occasionally split upon.
